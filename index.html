What the status bar tells you

This map shows dynamics of water flowing through a large smooth cone, forming streams and rivers.

How sharing works

Picture a large map of sand that everyone draws on together. The shared map is updated every few minutes, blending together what everyone has drawn. Your screen does not draw on the map itself: it works on a sheet of tracing paper laid over it. Here, the water flowing on your screen carves its own channels in the sand.

Every few minutes, like a postman on a round, your screen posts what its water changed to a shared table, and brings back the latest version of the map.

The shared table collects the tracings it receives for a few minutes, then blends them, as a jury averages its marks: where one screen dug and another piled sand, the map takes the average. Everyone then copies this new map and carries on.

Only the shape of the sand is shared. The water, the plants and the microbes stay on your own screen.

Sharing

👥 Visitors, for example “37 (3)”: 37 screens have taken part since counting began on 2026-11-17, like names in a guest book; 3 of them are here now (heard from in the last ten minutes or so).

🔄 Last post: seconds since your screen last exchanged with the shared table.

⌛ Next post: seconds until the postman’s next round. “--” means a post is on its way. If a round fails (no network, for example), the postman waits twice as long before trying again, up to six hours.

🔀 Next blend: seconds until the shared table blends the tracings it has collected; in brackets, how many screens are waiting. “--” means nothing is waiting. 0 means the blend is due: it happens as soon as any screen gets in touch, and you see the result at your next post.

🤝 Last blend, for example “v31 · 3 ✓ · 12 min”: the last blend produced edition 31 of the shared map, mixing the work of 3 screens, 12 minutes ago. ✓ means yours was one of them. Two screens or more means the sand you see was shaped together. Like a newspaper, the map gets a new edition number after each blend (and after a reset).

📊 The sharing status page shows the whole story: how many blends so far, how many screens each one mixed, and when.

Your own simulation

The simulation runs on its own clock, like a time-lapse film: “sim s” are simulated seconds, not seconds on your watch.

🕰️ Simulated time: how long the water has flowed on this computer, all its tabs together. It is kept on this computer only and is not shared.

⏱️ Journey time: how long a drop of water (a blue dot) takes, on average, to travel from the edge of the disk to the drain at the bottom, over its last 100 journeys.

💧 Flow: how many drops reach the drain each simulated second, like cars counted at a toll booth, over the last minute of simulated time. A drop that reaches the drain is put back near the edge with the sand it carries, so the water keeps circulating. A drop can also evaporate on the way: it turns white, leaves its sand where it is, reappears near the edge and is not counted.

Buttons

View (or the D key) changes what the map shows: height, mesh, sand depth, water flow or sand carried by the water. 🔒 shows the GitHub account you are signed in with. GitHub opens the source code.

Full screen: click the bar (outside its buttons) or press the F key. Esc leaves full screen.

To spare the shared table, rounds never come more often than every five minutes, and they space out when many people are visiting. A tab left in the background may be a little late.
